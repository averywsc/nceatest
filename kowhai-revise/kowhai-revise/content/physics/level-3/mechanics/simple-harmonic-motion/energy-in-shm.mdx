## Energy swaps, total stays the same

- With no friction the **total energy** is constant: $E = \tfrac{1}{2}kA^2$
- At the ends of the swing all the energy is potential: $E_p = \tfrac{1}{2}kA^2$ and $E_k = 0$
- At equilibrium all the energy is kinetic: $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}mv_{max}^2$
- At displacement $x$:
  - $E_p = \tfrac{1}{2}kx^2$
  - $E_k = \tfrac{1}{2}k\left(A^2 - x^2\right)$
  - speed $v = \omega\sqrt{A^2 - x^2}$

<Tips>

- Use energy to find the speed at a given displacement. It is quicker than using $\sin$ and $\cos$.
- Doubling the amplitude multiplies the total energy by four.

</Tips>
